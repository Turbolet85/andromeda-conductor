## Color Moods

### Mood 1: "Apollo Mission Control firing room, dark and counting"

**Physical space:** A single flight-controller station in a restored 1969-era MCC at night — a matte pale-green steel console front, a monochrome CRT showing phosphor-green characters on near-black, a hooded amber readout lamp, and one red GO/NO-GO indicator unlit until called. The room is low-glare on purpose: tan carpet, gray-green wallpaper, every surface chosen so the only bright things are the live readouts.

**Color palette (derived from this space):**
- Console Black `#0E0F10` — the unlit CRT glass and the matte instrument bezel that everything else sits against (near-black, not pure `#000`, to avoid halation — Linear's "Midnight" ground sits at this exact value).
- Phosphor Green `#3FB950` — the live telemetry characters glowing on the monochrome tube; the "nominal / on-timeline" status-light tier.
- Caution Amber `#D2A24C` — the hooded readout lamp and the amber glass over the warning annunciator; the "hold / operator-pause / CalibrationRegion" tier.

**Sensory anchor:** Everything is matte and absorbent except the three things that emit — green for nominal, amber for hold, a reserved red for NO-GO — so a verdict reads as a lit lamp on a dark panel, never as decoration. The pale-green steel of the console face is the only chromatic "furniture," keeping the surface feeling like equipment rather than a screen.

### Mood 2: "Bloomberg terminal desk at 3 a.m."

**Physical space:** A trading-floor terminal left running overnight on a dark desk — a deep charcoal-black screen packed with monospace numerics in signal-amber, dense rows of figures updating in place, no chrome, no imagery, just rate columns glowing against the dark. The desk around it is unlit; the terminal is the only light source.

**Color palette (derived from this space):**
- Terminal Charcoal `#16181C` — the dense data ground of the screen, a hair warmer and lighter than pure black so packed monospace rows stay legible without glare.
- Signal Amber `#E8A317` — the classic Bloomberg numeric glow; the dominant data ink for counters, P-IDs, and SLO timings, carrying the "armed and live" read.
- Quote Cyan `#4FC3DC` — the secondary tick color for changed/secondary values, a cool counterpoint that keeps the amber from going monotone.

**Sensory anchor:** The whole surface is built for one operator scanning dense numerics for hours — flat, low-chroma ground with amber as the single high-energy ink, so density reads as calm rather than busy. The lack of any decorative surface treatment is the point: it feels like an instrument that trusts you to read the trace.

### Mood 3: "k9s cluster watch on a Warp terminal"

**Physical space:** A modern terminal emulator filling a dark editor window — Warp's low-chroma gray ground with a k9s session inside it: a pinned status header tiering live resources green / yellow / red, a fixed keybinding hint row at the bottom, and discrete collapsible command blocks stacking down the screen as the run accrues. Cool-cast, technical, precise.

**Color palette (derived from this space):**
- Slate Ground `#1A1B26` — Warp/Tokyo-Night-style charcoal with a faint blue cast — the "cool-toned dark feels technical and precise" register for a developer-tool surface.
- Nominal Green `#7EE787` — the k9s "healthy" resource tier in the status header; Pass verdicts and on-timeline state.
- Degraded Yellow `#E3B341` — the k9s "degraded" amber that a resource turns *in place* under load; CalibrationRegion / operator-pause without alarm.

**Sensory anchor:** A cool blue-cast charcoal with a tight three-step status palette (green nominal / yellow degraded / red failing) makes the surface feel like live infrastructure rather than a document — color is functional state, never mood. The fixed header and pinned hint row keep the frame stable while counters stream, so even a Fail block expands without the view losing its footing.
