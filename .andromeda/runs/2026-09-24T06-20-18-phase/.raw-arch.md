## Patterns to follow
- The wrap amendment entry shape from the sidecar: `## {marker} — {title}` with `**Section:**` / `**Change:**` / `**Why:**`. Longer passes also carry a `**Sweep:**` block. The moved history should land in this shape (arch-amendments, e.g. `2026-09-22-interpretation-proven-live`).
- The "de-literalize to what the subject guarantees" precedent: a body claim is cut down to the precision the measurement supports, and the dated reading stays as history, not in the body (arch-amendments `2026-09-10-release-build-and-bundle`, second entry).
- The post-edit sweep discipline. After the last body edit, grep the retired or moved patterns across all seven masters plus the derived leaves, and record per-hit dispositions (arch-amendments `2026-09-17-a11y-routine-arm-terminal…` "Sweep (cascade step 2)" and `2026-09-22` "Sweep"). This is the model for a "no fact lost" check.
- Registry entries name the SET, not a tally literal ("No corpus tally literal was added — the registry names the SET", arch-amendments `2026-09-15-structurally-dead-assertion-class-retired`). This is a compaction-friendly form for count-bearing rows such as the `contracts/` member count and the declare-only family count.

## Anti-patterns to avoid
- **An amendment log in the body.** Dated "(added YYYY-MM-DD)", "RETIRED", "was X, now Y" and "measured at `{sha}`" narratives inside registry rows are the thing to move out. Replacing them with a body-side pointer list of dates recreates the log (arch-amendments preamble).
- **Silent semantic drift through summarization.** Examples: turning a scoped qualifier (per-posture, per-arm, dev-only, CI-job-scoped, "not a Conductor handle") into a universal, or merging the two OTLP service identities or the three suite families. Past amendments corrected exactly these errors (`2026-09-15`: two duplicate sites of one retired claim; `2026-09-22` (11): "identically" narrowed to deterministic posture).
- **Moving a registry row into §Standard Contracts or §Infrastructure Patterns** to get it under the cap. Those sections are out of scope, and Standard Contracts is already 15 486 B.
