## Patterns to follow
- Repository-hygiene step shape, per test-plan §6 Coverage-matrix completeness gate (static): each gate is its own `rust`-job step with a presence guard that emits `::error::` and exits 1, and has no `continue-on-error` and no `if:`. Keep this shape for every gate step moved to the Linux job.
- Can-fail proof in the suite, per test-plan §6 Coverage-matrix completeness gate (static): gates over committed data grade by exact-set equality in both directions, with no literal-count predicate, and show a negative arm that mutates the real artifact. This applies to the matrix-ledger gate re-based onto `coverage.md` (carries 5 and 6): its unique and no-P-NNN arms, and its fail-on-none arm across both the bolded and the bare id forms.
- Digest-pin discipline, per test-plan §6 Fingerprint-storm (fault-injection burst + read-back): the pin is over LF-normalized content, the grade runs only after the digest matches, and a one-byte tamper arm names the file and never the text. Keep each sha256 pin byte-identical when the captures move. Re-point only the path constants and `capture_population.rs`'s `read_dir`.
- In-memory fixture text, per test-plan §6 Coverage-matrix completeness gate (static): `workflow_env_gate` proves it can fail on workflow text built at test time, with no committed fixture. Its `runs-on: windows-latest` / `shell: pwsh` strings are parsed input, so restating or keeping them is P4's choice under that rule.

## Anti-patterns to avoid
- Lowering the coverage floor, or dropping or weakening any gate "just this once" during the runner move, per test-plan §11 Quality.
- Setting nextest `retries`, or running a live-Pulse leg as a CI gate on the new Linux runner, per test-plan §11 CI.
- Serializing tests with a runner knob (`--test-threads=1`, a profile setting) to absorb a Linux-only interleaving, per test-plan §11 Integration.
