## Goal

Settle why Pulse's storm detector reported `tracked_fingerprints_count: 0` while all nine of Conductor's
canary spans arrived and both sides agree on the attribute keys. The chunk's deliverable is a *settled
answer* plus the artifact that settles it — not a green fingerprint count. Either outcome closes it: if the
six exception spans do not reach Pulse's appender with their events intact, the defect is Conductor-side and
this entry owns the fix; if they do, the question moves to Pulse and this entry closes by handing it over.

P3 research corrected one expectation and confirmed the conclusion behind it. Pulse's
`tracked_fingerprints_count` is **a windowed gauge sampled after eviction, not a latched counter**
(`triage/src/pattern/storm.rs:370-415`): `run_one_storm_cycle` first drops every fingerprint whose timestamps
have aged past `DEFAULT_STORM_WINDOW_SECONDS = 60`, and only then reports `detector.fingerprints.len()` — a
count of **distinct** fingerprints, so a working six-occurrence identical-fingerprint storm reads `1`, never
`6`. The gauge alone therefore proves nothing about a sample taken after its window closed.

**The latched signals decide it, and they agree with the gauge.** `storms_detected_total` and
`fingerprints_evicted_total` (`:387-393`) are cumulative and immune to the window, and both were already
recorded at zero: `two-launch-verdict.md:187-189` has them "across every sample", and the operator's saved
**arm-3** tail carries a tick ~4 minutes *after* that arm's storm — postdating the whole canary window — with
both at 0 beside the gauge's 0. Cumulative counters reset per process, so nothing had entered arm 3's detector
feed by then; zero evictions means nothing ever aged out, i.e. nothing was ever tracked. Research also closed
one branch outright: the `FingerprintObserver` is genuinely wired (`main.rs:600-601`), not a null, and Pulse
reads the three keys from `event.attributes` exactly as Conductor writes them (`buffer/src/appender.rs:345-350`)
— the transcribed direction and arity are correct.

This **raises the prior on the arrival/intake half without saying which side owns it**: Conductor may not have
put intact events on the wire, or Pulse may not have taken them in. So the settling pair is unchanged — prove
what Conductor sends, and witness what arrives — but the weight shifts onto the Conductor-side wire proof,
because the Pulse-side reading has already returned its answer three times over.

So the work splits honestly along what each side can know. **Conductor's half** is proving what it actually
puts on the wire, which nothing proves today: the canary emission path has *no* wire-tier test, and the
shipped `emit.batch` attribute counts spans, not events (`conductor-emit/src/client.rs:67,74-81`), so it cannot
distinguish a span carrying an exception event from one that does not. **Pulse's half** is read from its own
storm tick, with the sampling window respected. Per obs-plan §1 `conductor-emit` is instrumentable at the
outbound gRPC boundary only, and per obs-plan §6 the sanctioned pattern for exactly this ambiguity — a reader
that degrades to empty, making divergence indistinguishable from emptiness — is a **shape witness recording
the observed KEY SET, names only, on the allowlisted `message` field**, the same amendment the read-back
boundary log took at the previous chunk.

## Implementation notes

- **The witness must not become the second silent zero.** The whole reason this chunk exists is a counter that
  read zero for an unexamined reason. A witness whose field is dropped by the allowlist, or whose level is
  filtered out at run time, reproduces the same failure one layer down — hence `message` (already allowlisted)
  and the explicit `RUST_LOG` item in the recipe. Verify the line appears in the real artifact, not in a test
  double.
- **The Conductor-side wire proof now carries the most weight.** Pulse's side has already answered three ways
  over (the gauge, `storms_detected_total`, `fingerprints_evicted_total` — all zero, including on a tick line
  postdating arm 3's entire canary window). What remains genuinely unknown is whether Conductor put intact
  events on the wire. If the step-3 test and the step-2 witness both show intact events, the hand-off to Pulse
  is well-evidenced even if the new live leg's tick reading adds nothing new; if they show a defect, it is
  Conductor's to fix and the Pulse-side zeros are fully explained.
- **A non-zero `fingerprints_evicted_total` on the new live leg would itself be news** — it would contradict
  the prior arms' pattern and mean something changed between legs. Record it prominently rather than treating
  the tick capture as a formality.
- **Do not let the metric correction over-reach.** The windowed-gauge finding fixes an *expectation* (a
  working storm reads 1, never 6) and nothing more; it does not soften the zero, because the cumulative
  counters were already zero across every sample. An earlier draft of this plan drew the opposite inference
  from the same finding without checking `two-launch-verdict.md:187-189` — the record is here so the reasoning
  is not re-run.
- **Order sensitivity in the live leg:** the storm lands behind the 45s warm-up and its detector window closes
  ~60s later. Tick lines read outside that span are not evidence about the storm. Capture the whole span, not
  a single line.
- **`emit_canary`'s observable behavior must not change** — same occurrence count, same seeding, same
  fingerprint. The refactor in step 3 is for testability only; the determinism-replay and preflight legs must
  stay byte-identical.
