## Goal

`crates/conductor-faults/tests/port_occupier.rs` violates the runner-portability contract that `test-plan`
§4 states and `architecture.md` §Established Decisions [Module Boundaries] makes possible: every crate green
under plain `cargo test -p <crate>` as well as under nextest. Measured at HEAD in P3: exit **101**, 6 of 7
integration tests passing.

The cause is not a diffuse race. The file holds **seven** `#[test]` fns in ONE binary. **Five** of the six
siblings construct a `PortOccupier` — `:12`, `:24`, `:34` (which calls `occupy` twice, the second
deliberately failing to a typed error, so it contributes one successful bind), `:43`, `:53` — while
`default_target_is_the_otlp_ingest_port` (`:62`) constructs nothing and merely asserts the constant. The
seventh, the witness, installs the process-global `init_observability` subscriber and constructs one
occupier **of its own** before reading the lines the subscriber writes. Under libtest's shared process every
one of those spans lands in that single sink — P3 measured **13 `fault.port_occupier` records in a 14-line
log, six `new` records carrying six distinct ephemeral ports** (the five siblings plus the witness's own) —
and the test's record finder takes the **first match**, which returned a sibling's port (60254). The failing
assertion is `the bound port survives the allowlist`. Under nextest's process-per-test there is exactly one
such record, which is why that runner has always been green.

`test-plan` §11 → Integration already names both the defect and its remedy, on a measured case in another
crate (`2026-08-20-verifier-self-hardening`): *"the remedy is a test binary of its own … never serializing
the file … which hides the shared-state defect rather than removing it."* The repo already applies it three
times — `conductor-run/tests/canary_obs_witness.rs:23`, `conductor-verify/tests/readback_shape_witness.rs:26`,
`conductor-timeline/tests/obs_span.rs:15`, each holding exactly one test fn (all three `#[tokio::test]`, not
plain `#[test]`). `port_occupier.rs` is the sole violator of a convention with three in-tree precedents. So this chunk applies the established remedy rather
than inventing one, and the assertion moves **byte-identically** — which is what preserves its power to fail
if `port` ever leaves `redact::ALLOWLISTED_FIELDS` (obs-plan §4; `"port"` confirmed present at
`redact.rs:51`).

The chunk's second half is non-optional and equally the point: the wrap gate that surfaced this

so the route entry's warning that a fix "may simply advance the first red" is **measured false**. A second

## Constraints & rejected approaches

- **Serializing the file** (`--test-threads=1`, a nextest profile knob, a `serial_test`-style lock) —
  banned by `test-plan` §11 → Integration as hiding the shared-state defect rather than removing it.
- **A retry, or pinning the crate to nextest** — banned by `test-plan` §10 and `architecture.md`
  §Infrastructure Patterns (the `ci` profile's zero-retry is arch-level determinism discipline), and
  independently by `obs-plan` §11 and `a11y-plan` §10.
- **An in-test `sleep`** to let the subscriber install settle — banned by `test-plan` §11 → E2E; the
  carve-out was proposed and explicitly REJECTED at `2026-09-06-operator-gated-live-suite`.
- **Keying the record finder on the port** (`find(|o| … && o["port"] == port)`) — this would make the
  suite green while rendering `assert_eq!(new.get("port"), Some(port))` tautological. `testing.md:89`: a
  probe that cannot fail on its property's negation is not evidence. **Do not resolve the defect this way.**
- **Changing `init_observability` to admit a non-global install** — a `conductor-core` change reaching every
  seam, to fix one test, when three sibling crates already solve it test-side. Out of scope.
- **Making the known-positive control a standing gate** — it mutates `redact.rs` and the wrap light gate
  re-runs gates verbatim; a source-mutating gate that aborts mid-run leaves the tree dirty. It is a
  one-shot /implement step with recorded evidence, guarded on every later run by the byte-identity diff.
