## Constraints & rejected approaches
- Relaxing the rule to its retry facet because the trigger line carries no scope — it weakens `v3-09`.
- Editing the rule between its markers to read the trigger line — the acceptance fixes byte-identity, and every prior
  series' recorded-rule arm would fail.
- A temp-rooted data dir — the committed chain masks no temp-rooted path, and a capture is written once.
- Extending the mask BEFORE d1 — it is not an input to the leg under a home-rooted dir, and a scrub-path edit ahead
  of the drives buys nothing but delay (inputs#I17).
- Editing the capture tool before d1 (to print `model_identity`, say) — the leg uses the capture as committed; the
  model's identity is read from Pulse's log into the ledger.
- A `boot` before any drive — it primes the incident the drive's own canary dedupes against.
- Launching with the WebKit rendering lever by default, or naming it in the contract section — the rebuilt app sets
  its own lever on Linux; the WebKit one is a single recorded relaunch, only if the app dies in its first 10 s
  (inputs#I18).
- Driving on the binaries already on disk — they predate `5f77859`.
- A Conductor reader of either model env handle, or a committed launch or census script under `scripts/` — the first
  is a new handle reader and the second a ninth governed form; both escalate. The pre-leg checks stay session-level.
- A fourth drive, a retry until a pass, or a re-fire of anything but a `pipeline-fault` canary.
- Splitting the three drives across two sittings, or any GPU drive at night.
- A mutation run — test-plan §9 owes none per chunk.
- Rewriting a prior section's moved Pulse coordinates — they are dated records; the new section re-pins.
- Unstaging, deleting or editing any frozen chunk's evidence.
